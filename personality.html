Personality & Learning

The way I think, the things I enjoy, and the people who inspire me all shape how I approach school and my future in technology.

How I learn

Three traits that shape my student life

01

Curiosity

I like finding out how things work, especially when I encounter a new technology or coding idea. That curiosity encourages me to ask questions, explore beyond the first explanation, and connect lessons to projects I can build.

Academic impact: It keeps me engaged and helps me take initiative when a topic is unfamiliar.

02

Perseverance

Learning software development involves mistakes and repeated attempts. When a program or project does not work, I try to identify the problem, make a change, and learn from the result instead of giving up.

Academic impact: It helps me work through challenging assignments and build understanding step by step.

03

Creativity

I enjoy turning ideas into something practical, such as a website or interface. Building projects gives me a way to experiment with design and apply what I learn in HTML, CSS, and JavaScript.

Academic impact: It makes lessons more meaningful because I can use concepts to create and improve real work.

A hobby that brings these together: I enjoy coding and building web projects. It gives me room to explore, persist through bugs, and make an idea visible.

An inspiration

Grace Hopper

Grace Murray Hopper (1906–1992) was a mathematician, U.S. Navy officer, and computing pioneer. She worked on the Harvard Mark I, helped develop early compiler technology, and advocated for making programming more accessible. Her career connected deep technical work with a willingness to challenge assumptions.

“The most dangerous phrase in the language is, ‘We’ve always done it this way.’”

The moment I admire is Hopper continuing to argue for programming languages that people could read and use, even when others doubted the idea. Her example reminds me that learning technology is not only about mastering what already exists; it is also about asking whether a better approach is possible. As a student, I want to bring that same openness to difficult problems and keep learning when an idea seems unfamiliar.

Why she inspires me

  • She paired technical skill with imagination.
  • She kept learning and contributing across a long career.
  • She challenged conventions to make computing more useful.

Background and quote: U.S. Naval History and Heritage Command, “Code, Commission, and Character: The Unstoppable Career of Rear Adm. Grace Hopper”.

A student reflection

LLMs: A Tool, Not a Crutch

To me, this statement suggests that I should take responsibility for my learning. An AI tool can help me to think but it cannot do the work for me of understanding, checking and expressing ideas to myself.

As a student, I can use large language models such as ChatGPT, Claude, and Copilot to clarify a difficult concept, Harvard University states that the use of AI in Computer Science should generally be around brainstorming questions, generate practice exercises, or receive feedback on an outline or a piece of code (Harvard University & Generative AI @ Harvard, 2026). I can use them before class to review unfamiliar terms, during independent study to get hints when I am stuck, and after class to test whether I can explain a topic in my own words. For programming, I can ask for a hint about an error, then trace the code and test a solution myself.

The danger of AI as a crutch is that I may get an assignment done without doing the exercise that increases expertise. UNESCO, (Guidance for Generative AI in Education and Research, 2026) advocates for a human-centered approach to generative AI in education, whereas Harvard’s guidance on student use supports critical examination and appropriate use. I read these notions as saying that the technology should help me study more actively, not cover up gaps in my comprehension. My test is simple: After employing AI, can I describe the answer and how I arrived at it? If I can’t, then I have some learning to do.”
